# Volume I — the formal statements

Sean Brady, originator and conceptual director, 2026-10-29. Spelling checked before recording; wording his.

## The sentence of SAM

**"Matter displaces space, and that displacement accumulates."**

## The SAM law

**"The farther you look out into space, the more accumulation you are looking through."**

The SAM law is not a challenge to Hubble's law. It offers a different explanation for the same phenomena.

## The floor, A₀ (Sean Brady, 2026-10-30; spelling and grammar checked, wording his)

The idea of SAM is that space (the substrate) is made of something, and SAM attempts to apply a value to it. A₀ is theorised as the minimum value for space itself to exist. If matter displaces space and that displacement accumulates, then, like land above sea level, it lifts from a floor, and that floor is A₀.

Clarifications given the same day: a constant value greater than zero everywhere has no gradient, no matter how high it is, so the floor defines nothing to move against; it is the difference in the value of A that matters. A₀ is huge compared with the A generated by the Earth. The Earth, the Sun and everything in the Milky Way produce accumulation, so the floor stands alone only in the vastest and emptiest regions of the universe.

## Where they sit in Volume I

The sentence names the three core objects: A₀ is the undisplaced space; A(r) is the displacement of one source; the accumulation is the displacement adding up along a line of sight (the road operator), with the ceiling 1/π at the end of the longest road. The SAM law is the sentence read along a line of sight: redshift with distance as accumulation looked through, offered beside Hubble's law as an explanation of the same observations, not against them.

These are the opening statements of the reworked Chapter I; the aquarium thought experiment (2023) is their picture and stands as envisioned.

## The two thought experiments, in Sean Brady's words (spelling checked, wording his)

**The aquarium (2023).** An indestructible aquarium the size of a room, filled completely with water, with no room to add a single atom and no possibility for a single atom to escape. A small spherical rock the size of a softball grows into existence in the middle. With the water having nowhere to go, a halo of "denser" water forms around the rock. A small pebble floating in a straight line towards the rock enters the halo, slows, and its path bends around the rock; once it leaves the halo it continues in a straight path as before. The pebble floats in "something", and when the value of that something is changed (matter displaces space) the pebble behaves differently. The picture stands as envisioned and is not to be changed to fit known work.

**The magic bell.** On one large planet near a supermassive black hole sits a mirror clock with one observer. On a second, tiny planet in a void sits only an observer with a magic bell that rings exactly when light strikes the mirror. The question is: does the observer hear the bell and see the light strike the mirror in unison? The question is not to be answered in the volume. The bell challenges the idea that an observer is granted a real-time view of distant events, but that was not the original intent; it was the result of a question: how can accumulation be reconciled with the need to adjust the clocks in GPS satellites? Hence the clock and the road.

**The result of the magic bell, in Sean Brady's words (without answering the question):** if A is not equal at two endpoints, accumulation must exist between them.
