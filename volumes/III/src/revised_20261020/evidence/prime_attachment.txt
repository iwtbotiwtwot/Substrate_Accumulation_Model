      Reliability and resource tradeoffs in hidden-spin feedback for prime-sequence
                                       computation
                                                          Sean Brady∗
                                                    Independent Researcher
                                                  (Dated: September 27, 2026)
                We investigate a seven-spin stochastic gate coupled to an event-created digital sieve, separating local
             failure probabilities, whole-trajectory reliability, and the resources used to improve them. Integrating
             out six hidden spins gives two composite-blocking margins, b and 2a − b. A source change chosen to
             equalize these margins raises the observed fraction of exact prime/composite trajectories through
             109 from 1/64 to 47/64 per accumulation profile; 13/16 fresh seeds are also exact. The intervention
             reduces one-bank false emissions by about a factor of 63 but increases two-bank false emissions by
             about a factor of 64 and raises the microscopic strength budget. Reanalysis of the archived error
             counts gives Poisson zero-error predictions of 0.012 and 0.732 for baseline and balanced sources, with
             dispersion close to unity. A separate ideal-input census and finite-state calculation predict a balanced
             zero-error probability near 0.717 without fitting trajectory outcomes. Matched-budget controls show
             that equalizing worst-case margins is not a universal optimum for trajectory survival: uniformly
             strengthening the unbalanced source is favored when hidden fields are included in the strength budget,
             whereas the ranking reverses for a coupling-only budget. Four-reading confirmation at unchanged
             total exposure provides another competitive control. These results delimit the original intervention
             and identify the resource and opportunity weights required for reliable stochastic sequence design.
