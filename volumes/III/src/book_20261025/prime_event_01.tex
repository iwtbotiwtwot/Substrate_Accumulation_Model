\subsection{The object being constructed}

The Prime Event program joins two explicit constructions. A finite Ising source decides whether to emit at an integer candidate, while accepted emissions create recurrence channels that become later inputs to that same source. Its mathematical interest lies in the join: a conditional boundary interaction becomes a probabilistic gate, the gate changes its future input history, and hidden source state survives between decisions. The completed work now includes a measured transfer from large exact spin responses into newly compiled gates, a substantial reduction in conditional leakage, raw unrepaired execution, source-derived activity clocks, and an exact correction for clock information omitted by an accurate event-only reduction. These are different results on linked, explicitly identified objects.\samref{V3PE01}\samref{V3PE02}

The seven-spin gate must first be distinguished from the large graphs used to obtain boundary responses. Its two Boolean bank inputs are clamped; its output and six hidden spins are free. For each input pair it therefore has exactly $2^7=128$ microscopic states. A large-$N$ response can supply design coefficients for a new gate without transferring the large graph's dynamics. Conversely, a successful gate coefficient does not by itself supply the recurrence channels. This separation makes the measured spin-to-prime connection precise: exact equilibrium source features inform a new microscopic model, whose finite-rate response is then evaluated with its own state space and clock.\samref{V3PE03}

The original source design is attributable to the project lineage recorded in the reports: Sean Brady supplied the concept and research direction; ChatGPT and Codex collaborated on the constructions. Codex supplied the selective compiler examples, the finite dynamics and controls, the balance and leakage derivations, and the clock and memory deductions described below. The connecting exposition in this chapter is manuscript synthesis of those completed sources; it adds no scientific calculation.

\subsection{A selected interaction from pairwise microscopic edges}

Write the output as $e=\pm1$ and the return inputs as $r_1,r_2=\pm1$, with $+1$ meaning that a bank returns. Let
\[
 \mathcal P_+=\{+++,+--,-+-,--+\}.
\]
Two hidden spins $h_1,h_2$ provide pair modules. Four more, $z_p$ indexed by $p\in\mathcal P_+$, provide a field-bearing triple module. The retained Hamiltonian is
\[\begin{aligned}
 E={}&-u h_1(e+r_1)-u h_2(e+r_2)\\
 &-t\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_+}z_p
       (1+p_1e+p_2r_1+p_3r_2).
\end{aligned}\]
Every microscopic term is a pair interaction or an explicit hidden field. The visible three-spin term is created by eliminating hidden variables. The fields are essential: a zero-field Ising source has global inversion symmetry, whereas an odd visible interaction requires that symmetry to be broken by a field or by a pinned boundary.\samref{V3PE01}\samref{V3PE02}

For one pair module, hidden summation gives
\[
 \sum_{h=\pm1}e^{\beta u h(e+r)}
       =2\cosh[\beta u(e+r)].
\]
The local field has magnitude $2u$ when $e=r$ and vanishes otherwise. Thus its free energy is a constant minus $aer$, with
\[
 a(\beta,u)=\frac{\log\cosh(2\beta u)}{2\beta}.
\]
For the four-spin hidden module, the absolute local fields are $4t,0,0,0$ when $er_1r_2=+1$, and $2t,2t,2t,2t$ when it is $-1$. Consequently
\begin{align*}
 Z_{3,+}&=16\cosh(4\beta t),\\
 Z_{3,-}&=16\cosh(2\beta t)^4,\\
 b(\beta,t)&=\frac{4\log\cosh(2\beta t)
                    -\log\cosh(4\beta t)}{2\beta}.
\end{align*}
The complete marginal has the selected form
\begin{align*}
 F(e\mid r_1,r_2)&=C(r_1,r_2)+eH(r_1,r_2),\\
 H(r_1,r_2)&=-a(r_1+r_2)+b r_1r_2.
\end{align*}
Here the input-only constant is harmless for conditional output probabilities, but is not a statement that the full thermodynamic source has zero offset.\samref{V3PE02}

This compiler was obtained by more than choosing a favorable truth table. Its predecessor enumerated all 67 four-boundary sign subgroups and showed that a single subgroup projection retains an interaction family closed under XOR. Retaining two disjoint pairs therefore also permits their four-spin product; retaining two overlapping pairs permits the remaining pair. Unequal nonnegative copies of gauge-transformed stars can remove unwanted companions. The search examined 6,560 nonempty copy vectors with multiplicities zero, one or two, and its derivation extended the relevant minima to arbitrary nonnegative integer multiplicities within that dictionary. Independent local modules then allowed the two pair strengths and triple orientation to be selected separately. Exact character cancellation establishes all-temperature \emph{support}; the numerical coefficients still depend on temperature. The theorem fixes interaction support at all temperatures; amplitude matching is performed at its declared temperature.\samref{V3PE01}

The accepting output is $e=-1$, so
\[
 \Pr(e=-1\mid r_1,r_2)
       =\frac{1}{1+e^{-2\beta H(r_1,r_2)}}.
\]
The three distinct fields are $2a+b$ with no returning bank, $-b$ with exactly one, and $-2a+b$ with both. The desired static truth table requires $a>0$ and $0<b<2a$. Its two blocking margins are therefore $b$ and $2a-b$. Exactly one bank is a revealing sector: $r_1+r_2=0$, so the two pair contributions cancel. Increasing only the pair strength cannot improve that sector's equilibrium suppression. This elementary cancellation later explains the actual microscopic first-error data.

\subsection{From a driven response to an event-created sieve}

The first dynamic construction drove the source with externally specified recurrence periods. It retained the complete 128-state distribution, used continuous-time heat-bath switching, and compared corrugated traversal intervals with uniform and rough accumulation profiles. In the column convention, neighboring states have rates
\[
 L_{yx}=\frac{\Gamma}{1+e^{\beta(E_y-E_x)}},
 \qquad L_{xx}=-\sum_{y\ne x}L_{yx}.
\]
The exposure of a supplied interval is proportional to $\Gamma d_k\sqrt{1-A_k}$. Matching the step mean of $A$ and the total exposure does not fix the response to an ordered sequence of changing bank inputs. For periods $5/7$, pair strength two and $\Gamma=8$, balanced correctness was $71.737824\%$ under uniform accumulation, $71.236764\%$ under exposure-matched rough accumulation, and $71.525318\%$ when the same rough values were reversed. At fast relaxation the ordering difference fell below numerical comparison resolution. The rough profile could improve or worsen the response according to its alignment with the inputs. These completed driven comparisons establish a finite-memory mechanism, with clamping and accumulation supplied as drives; their work and backreaction were not assigned by that experiment.\samref{V3PE04}

The feedback successor makes the periods event-created. Begin at candidate $n=2$ with no channels. Every accepted event at $n$ installs a channel of period $n$, with first return at $2n$ and subsequent returns at $3n,4n,\ldots$. Channels are assigned alternately to two banks in creation order. A bank input is the OR of its channels returning at the current candidate. The ideal decision rule accepts exactly when neither bank returns.\samref{V3PE05}

That ideal rule generates primes by induction. If all previous accepted events are the primes below $n$, a prime $n$ has no earlier prime divisor and no channel returns. A composite has a smaller prime divisor $p$ and hence a returning channel at $n=mp$, $m\ge2$. The induction depends on retaining the whole accepted history, rather than periodically replacing it with a list supplied by a primality oracle. The implemented probabilistic gate can make mistakes, and each mistaken emission creates a real channel under the same rule.

An extra composite channel never directly returns at a later prime: every return $mp$ with $m\ge2$ is composite even if its period $p$ was itself composite. It can nevertheless alter future decisions by changing creation-order parity, bank assignment, and the carried microscopic state. A missed prime removes a period that could have blocked later composites. These mechanisms make feedback a history-dependent source process, rather than a set of independent truth-table samples. The integer counter, the event-created periods and alternating bank rule remain explicit parts of the algorithm; the spin source has not derived that integer recurrence from autonomous matter dynamics.\samref{V3PE02}\samref{V3PE05}

Three confirming readings reduce leakage while preserving the source trajectory. If $T_n$ is the propagator for one third of the declared exposure and $D$ projects onto output $-1$, then
\[
 P_{\rm acc}(n\mid\rho)
   =\mathbf1^\top D T_n D T_n D T_n\rho.
\]
The incoming vector $\rho$ contains all seven spins. A sampled trajectory keeps its actual state after every reading and after both accepted and rejected candidates. The formula is a masked matrix contraction, not the cube of an equilibrium output probability. The three readings share the same bank input at a candidate, since a channel created there cannot return until a later candidate. Uniform and rough production profiles retain 35 phases and matched total exposure; their phase ordering remains available to influence transient state.\samref{V3PE02}

At fixed pair coefficient $a$, balancing the two blocked margins means maximizing $\min(b,2a-b)$. Its solution is $b=a$. This criterion was selected before observing successor seed outcomes. At $\beta=1$ define $C=\cosh(2u)$. Solving the analytic marginal gives

 t(u)=\frac12\operatorname{acosh}

For $u=4$, the four triple rows and their hidden fields change from strength two to $2.3464897298984759$; the pair rows remain four. The topology and 128-state space stay fixed. Every indexed spin graph had zero local fields. All 91,620 odd-degree coefficient intervals contained zero, as required by global inversion.  \beta F(e,r_1,r_2\mid p)
 =\text{input terms}+e(d+ur_1+vr_2+wr_1r_2).
\]
The four-body term becomes the visible triple $w$; the pair joining output to the pinned port becomes the output bias $d$. \subsection{Uniform leakage improvement and the first-error theorem}

A separate continuation increased the balanced source strength while retaining the same readout. At $u=5$, the analytic balance gives $t=2.8465622403941495$ and $a=b=4.653426410750604$. Temperature remains one, attempt exposure remains $\Gamma=512$, and there are still three readings. Under balance, the no-bank field is $3a$ and every blocked input field is $-a$. Independent equilibrium readings suggest an error scale near $e^{-6a}$; this explains the design direction, while the actual result uses the complete finite-rate sampler.\samref{V3PE09}\samref{V3PE10}

All $35\times4\times128=17,920$ phase/bank/state inputs strictly improved. The worst blocked-input emission probability fell from $3.04422969\times10^{-10}$ to $8.0581104\times10^{-13}$; worst no-bank suppression fell from $9.06033914\times10^{-10}$ to $2.250089\times10^{-12}$. The minimum improvement across individual inputs exceeded $377.78$. These are probabilities of the encoded CDF sampler. Its binary64 transition tables were independently compared with matrix exponentials, but exact finite-table counting has its own precise meaning.\samref{V3PE09}

Let $L=2^{53}$ and let $C_s(j)$ be a stored row CDF. The number of uniform-grid draws selecting state $j$ is
\[
 W_{sj}=\lceil LC_s(j)\rceil-\lceil LC_s(j-1)\rceil,
\]
with endpoints clipped to the draw range. Each row sums to $L$. Set $V_0(s)=1$ and
\[
 V_{k+1}(s)=\sum_{j:\,e(j)=-1}W_{sj}V_k(j).
\]
Then the exact three-reading emission probability is $V_3(s)/L^3$, with denominator $2^{159}$. This counts retained-state paths through all three readings. It establishes exactness for the encoded sampler under independent uniform-grid draws, rather than asserting that rounded tables equal exact real exponentials.\samref{V3PE10}

The conditional table also supports a useful finite first-error theorem. Suppose accepted history is exactly the primes before the next candidate, and fresh draw triples are independent uniform-grid draws conditional on the entire past. Let $c$ bound blocked-input emission over all phases and incoming states, and $d$ bound no-bank suppression. The induction above assigns a nonzero mask to every composite and mask zero to every prime, regardless of the microscopic preparation. Disjointly decomposing by the position of the first error gives
\begin{align*}
 \Pr(\text{an error through }N)
 &\le [N-1-\pi(N)]c+\pi(N)d,
\end{align*}
with the bound capped at one. Dependence of incoming states across candidates is allowed. The hypothesis concerns new conditional draws; a fixed PCG64 seed is a realized deterministic algorithmic path. After an error, accepted periods can be wrong, so this proof is a first-error bound and does not bound every subsequent error in a corrupted feedback history.\samref{V3PE10}

\subsection{Native clocks retain information beyond accepted indices}

The clock continuation returns to the balanced seven-spin source with its retained kinetic policy, $\beta=1$, $\Gamma=512$, $\eta=3/8$, and actual source-time observation schedule. It does not treat the $u5$ production horizon as a new clock experiment. For the actual state $S_t$ and bank $b$, let $\lambda_b$ be the sum of all seven flip rates and $\kappa_b$ the output-spin flip rate. Two native activity coordinates are
\[
 \tau_{\rm all}(t)=\int_0^t\lambda_b(S_s)\,ds,
 \qquad
 \tau_{\rm out}(t)=\int_0^t\kappa_b(S_s)\,ds.
\]
The compensated flip counts are martingales under the source process. Their usual expectation identity applies to the declared unconditioned or appropriately stopped horizon; it does not automatically hold after selection on a future accepted history.\samref{V3C04}\samref{V3PE17}

For any positive activity $a$, time change gives the column generator $Q^a=Q\operatorname{diag}(1/a)$ and speed measure proportional to $a\pi$, where $\pi$ is stationary for $Q$. Dividing by the output hazard makes the output flip rate exactly one from every microscopic state. Therefore, if $C_e$ groups only the visible output,
\[
 C_eQ^{\rm out}=H C_e,
 \qquad
 H=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This is an exact two-state output quotient in output-activity time, for every bank and hidden preparation. Its apparent simplicity does not remove the source's memory. The actual readings occur at random stopping coordinates $\tau_{\rm out}(t_j)$ inherited from source-time waits. Substituting equal clock waits changes the protocol. The all-spin activity clock does not even have this output-only quotient, because $\kappa/\lambda$ varies within each output sector.\samref{V3PE17}

The full accepted clock law uses the tilted kernel
\[
 F_b(z,t)=\exp[(Q_b-z\operatorname{diag}\kappa_b)t].
\]
The masked product $\mathbf1^\top D F_3 D F_2 D F_1\rho$ is the clock-weighted acceptance mass. Divide by its value at $z=0$ to obtain the conditional Laplace transform. Normalization and all three masks are part of the observable.

At bank zero, initial states 24 and 102 both show output $-1$ and have acceptance mass $0.999999999093968$. Their conditional transforms at $z=1$ are nevertheless $0.9999998550562574$ and $0.2252263286074421$. Directed mean-clock bounds put the first mean below $0.000141473371361$ and the second above $1.49064947744$, a separation of at least $1.490508004067729$ activity units. Bank-one Laplace values also differ, but its displayed mean intervals overlap and do not order those means. These are declared hidden preparations, not equal-frequency production populations.\samref{V3C04}

A finite raw-feedback experiment ran 32 fresh seeds through candidate 2048: 65,504 decisions, 23,256,750 actual flips and 54,138 output flips. Each seed accepted the same 309 events without errors, yet all 31 comparison seeds had different internal clock coordinates from the first. Doubling every rate while halving every laboratory wait reproduced states, readings, bank history and both clocks bitwise. Its units are activity; a physical lapse or calibration in seconds was not supplied by this construction.\samref{V3C04}

\subsection{An accurate event quotient can omit clock memory}

The next completed comparison retained output and hidden bits 5 and 6, giving eight coarse coordinates. Let $C$ group these blocks and $R$ lift each block using its declared conditional Gibbs weights. Then $CR=I_8$, $P=RC$ is a projection, and the omitted residual has dimension 120. The stored rate model is bound as exact binary rationals with diagonals rebuilt exactly; conditional weights are normalized within blocks. This does not silently assert that the stored Gibbs weights are stationary for the rounded generator.\samref{V3PE18}\samref{V3PE19}

For a masked, possibly tilted interval operator $T$, decompose the full state as $x=Ru+v$. The exact update is
\begin{align*}
 u'&=CTR u+CTv,\\
 v'&=(I-P')T(Ru+v).
\end{align*}
The signed injection $CTv$ is the source information lost by replacing the residual with zero. This is the same structural issue treated for spin preparation and selected readout in Chapter~\ref{v3-response-composition}, now evaluated on the actual prime gate and clock.

For three readings, set $T_i=D F_b(z,d_i)$ using the actual source-time waits $d_i$, and write
\begin{align*}
 G_{\rm full}&=CT_3T_2T_1,\\
 G_8&=CT_3PT_2PT_1P.
\end{align*}
The exact difference is $G_{\rm full}-G_8=\Delta_0+\Delta_1+\Delta_2$, with
\begin{align*}
 \Delta_0&=CT_3PT_2PT_1(I-P),\\
 \Delta_1&=CT_3PT_2(I-P)T_1,\\
 \Delta_2&=CT_3(I-P)T_2T_1.
\end{align*}
These are respectively the preparation replacement, first-reading reset and second-reading reset. Keeping the full first interval removes only $\Delta_0$. Adding all three signed operators restores the evaluated full response for every preparation; it is not an autonomous eight-state production algorithm.\samref{V3PE19}

For the retained bank-zero protocol, the ordinary eight-coordinate endpoint discrepancy was at most $6.175\times10^{-20}$ in $L^1$, while the clock-weighted discrepancy was about $0.774772$. States 102 and 120 share all retained coordinates, yet their conditional Laplace responses differ by $0.7747725033$. Any initializer using only those coordinates must therefore miss at least one response by $0.3873862517$.
