Scope of this edition
This volume is a source-bound account of the work already available. Its purpose is to explain the constructions, preserve their
useful results and failed controls, and state the limits that remain. A chapter’s open questions identify the boundary of the record;
they are not conditions that must all be solved to complete this manuscript.
Four levels of claim. Algebraic identities follow from their displayed definitions. Conditional theorems follow from explicit
hypotheses. Historical computation and empirical comparisons are described with their recorded operands, readout conventions and
limits. Physical identifications require a separate bridge to measured systems; a matching count or decimal does not supply that
bridge by itself.
Versioned history. The edition uses frozen SAMA manuscripts and the source authorities identified in the chapter references.
Different branches can record different development stages. A named engine is described at its documented revision, rather than
asserted to be the currently installed system. Missing promotion, deployment or custody records remain qualified instead of being
replaced by newly invented evidence.
Verification scope. This assembly did not launch native research campaigns, retraining, prime searches, binding fits, or new
experimental comparisons. Historical validation counts are preserved as historical reports. Editorial checks, typesetting checks and
explicitly identified algebra checks are narrower than an independent replication of all source experiments.
Navigation. There is one contents list and a continuous page sequence. Chapters use Roman identifiers; internal sections,
equations, figures and tables identify their chapter. Source references and the reproducibility register at the end retain the
provenance needed to distinguish source history from the present exposition.
Relation to the neighboring volumes. Volume I treats accumulation, clocks, signals and physical records. Volume II treats
the admission and organization of finite matter, its mass and binding readouts, and its source return. Volume III treats the exact
computational grammar, executable histories and applications. Receiving an object from another volume does not silently prove its
physical interpretation.

Reader’s contract and source boundary
This volume records the computational part of SAM: a typed grammar, exact finite state spaces, complete writes, retained histories,
and the applications that receive them. It is a source-grounded synthesis of the retained research, not a report of new numerical
campaigns. The exact objects are worth stating independently of the research that remains unfinished.
Three evidential levels must remain distinct. An algebraic identity is established under its displayed definitions and assumptions.
An executed result is reported through its retained source artifact and stated validation. A physical interpretation or proposed
continuation requires its own receiving argument and evidence. Historical validation counts and timing measurements in this
volume are preserved reports, not fresh independent reruns performed while preparing this book.
The principal source is the SAMA public repository at commit 4c2b5dc1649da5900c90b90d0ce56c223dbb7717,
dated 8 September 2026. The continuous parent and fourteen topic manuscripts are retained separately with their file hashes.
The topic manuscripts contain later amendments that the continuous parent does not always include. This edition follows those
amendments where they are explicit and identifies their source-reported scope.
Version language needs particular care. The retained August Library volume describes v1.2. The older public SAM Research
Project live pages describe SLCV21R. The later SAMA selector chapter describes SLCQ2-RZ with SLCV21R delegated arithmetic.
Still later application passages describe the RH-local Q3RHV2 and bounded CEV1/MP successors; those are not automatic changes
to the global selector. The Q2 promotion and candidate result paths cited by SAMA were not retrievable from the older public
repository during preparation. Consequently, every statement of installed or current authority below reports the identified source’s
state, not a fresh verification of a live machine or service.
Volume I supplies the declared accumulation, route and closure semantics. Volume II supplies the typed rows, carriers, connectors
and receiving structure. Their physical assumptions are not re-proved by compiling them. In particular, source support is not
automatically an energy coefficient, a carrier is not a matter row, and a completed receipt is not an additional independent state
variable.
The book retains failed controls and successful successors together. It makes no new claim to prove RH, establish universal
computational acceleration, calibrate physical energy, or realize a new physical computer. Proposed extensions in the closing
chapter are labeled as proposals. They are part of an honest account of where the work stands, rather than conditions that must be
solved before the existing work can be written down.

energy, but how many assignments occupy every possible energy. For a declared binary Hamiltonian this complete answer is

couplings and fields.

component convolution, modular polynomial arithmetic, orbit sectors and rank-aware readout are different routes to the

agreement, state- count closure, moments, symmetries, port
marginals, hashes and independent readers. Runtime is useful
evidence about an execution route; it is not the definition of
exactness.
