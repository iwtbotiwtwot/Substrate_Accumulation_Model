Let \(J(b)=\int_0^\infty(1-\cos bx)x^{-2}dx\). Integration by parts and
the Dirichlet integral give \(J(b)=\pi|b|/2\). The latter integral follows
by first inserting \(e^{-\epsilon x}\), differentiating with respect to
\(b\), and integrating \(\epsilon/(\epsilon^2+b^2)\) from zero to \(b\);
the result is \(\arctan(b/\epsilon)\), tending to \(\pi/2\) for \(b>0\).
Integration-by-parts tail bounds justify this Abel limit. The convergent
combination of the three \(J\)'s therefore yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int_0^\infty p(x)\cos(zx)dx
 &=\frac{J(z+2)+J(z-2)-2J(z)}{2\pi}\\
 &=\frac{|z+2|+|z-2|-2|z|}{4}
 =\left(1-\frac{|z|}{2}\right)_+.
\end{aligned}\tag{4}
\]

At \(z=0\) this also proves the normalization in (3). The construction
retains the hard-shell ultraviolet tail and the nonzero infrared density.

Write the formal equilibrium displacement
\(h_a=g_a/(I_*\omega)=a/\sqrt{2I_*\omega}\). For a normal coherent state
with amplitude \(f\) of finite free energy, square completion is an identity

\[
 \langle F_a\rangle_f

 \tag{5}
\]

The notation does not assert \(h_a\) belongs to the one-particle Hilbert

ultraviolet endpoint \(g_a\) itself need not be in \(\mathfrak h\); (1)
should not be silently interpreted as a smeared creation operator on the

in (5), its closed energy-form realization, and finite-time normal Weyl

## 2. Forced solution and exact residual — C01

Let \(\mu\) be a finite real signed Borel measure on \([0,T]\) with
\(\mu([0,T])=1\). Thus \(s(t)=\mu([0,t])\) is a bounded-variation real
scalar control, with value zero before zero and one after \(T\). Endpoint
atoms are included. Bounded variation is the meaning of finite signed ramp
here; distributions of infinite total variation are outside this theorem.

For the clean normal-Fock case, start in the free vacuum, with the field
coupling off, and supply the force \(s(t)a_1\) of one fixed legal endpoint
source. Intermediate force strengths are external actuator settings. They
are not newly legal fractional C4 matter states. The classical scalar
force fixes the coherent amplitude through

\[
 \dot f_t=-i\omega f_t+i\omega s(t)h_{a_1},\qquad f_{0-}=0.
 \tag{6}
\]

Integration by parts in the Stieltjes sense, including both endpoint
atoms, gives at and after the terminal drive change

\[
 f_T=h_{a_1}\left[1-e^{-i\omega T}\widehat\mu(\omega)\right],
 \qquad \widehat\mu(\omega)=\int_{[0,T]}e^{i\omega u}\mu(du).
 \tag{7}
\]

For example, \(\mu=\delta_T\) leaves \(f_T=0\): a final instantaneous
force change does not move the field. An atom at zero has the full elapsed
evolution. Both contribute to the residual relative to the final force.

Equation (7) is a normal finite-energy coherent amplitude. Indeed,

\[
 1-e^{-i\omega T}\widehat\mu(\omega)
 =\int[1-e^{-i\omega(T-u)}]\mu(du)
\]

is bounded by \(\|\mu\|_{\rm TV}\min(2,\omega T)\). This suppresses the
infrared singularity of \(h\), while \(p(x)/x=O(x^{-3})\) controls the
ultraviolet photon norm. Its energy is finite because \(p\) is integrable.
The associated Weyl displacement is unitary on normal Fock space. Its scalar
phase does not change (5); it must still be retained when comparing coherent
source histories or transported Writes.

For a transition between two legal nonzero endpoint drives, supplied in the
equilibrium energy-form sector of \(a_0\), the same oscillator calculation
has deviation \(-e^{-i\omega T}\Delta h\widehat\mu\) from \(h_{a_1}\).
Consequently in either precisely stated case,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_{\rm res}
 &=D_{\rm jump}\int_0^\infty p(x)
       \left|\int e^{ixt/\tau}\mu(dt)\right|^2dx\\
 &=D_{\rm jump}\iint
       \left(1-\frac{|t-u|}{2\tau}\right)_+\mu(dt)\mu(du).
\end{aligned}\tag{8}
\]

The real signed condition ensures that the imaginary antisymmetric cross
terms cancel; the two ordered real mixed terms remain. Fubini is valid:
the absolute integral is bounded by \(\|\mu\|_{\rm TV}^2\int p\).
It is an exact whole-continuum energy-form identity, not an assertion from
sampling the spectrum. In particular no photon cutoff is used by (8).
The normalized ratio is used when \(D_{\rm jump}>0\), as for the supplied
nontrivial source/Gray displacement. If \(\Delta a=0\), the unnormalized
identity gives \(E_{\rm res}=0\); no strictly positive energy is asserted
for a zero drive. The normalized control functional below remains defined
independently of that trivial source case.

An arbitrary normalized incoming state cannot replace the specified vacuum
or equilibrium sector. For a coherent incoming amplitude \(f_0\), the
general deviation at the end is

\[
 f_T-h_{a_1}=e^{-i\omega T}
 \big[(f_0-h_{a_0})-\Delta h\widehat\mu(\omega)\big].
 \tag{9}
\]

Its square includes the incoming energy and its signed cross term with the
drive. For general states the corresponding mean, covariance and energy
form are required. An equilibrium Gaussian sector with fluctuations retains
their energy/reference separately; (8) is the additional coherent loading
excess, not its total thermal energy. Thus “a normalized finite-energy
preparation” alone is insufficient to infer (8).

## 3. Sharp lower for all finite signed ramps — C02

Put \(a=2\tau\), \(L=T/a\), and write \(L=n+r\) with integer \(n\geq0\)
and \(0\leq r<1\). The alternate endpoint representation \(r=1\) is also
valid and gives the same value as \((n+1,0)\). Let

\[
 m(x)=\mu((x-a,x]),\qquad K_a(v)=(1-|v|/a)_+.
\]

The intersection of two intervals of length \(a\) has length
\((a-|t-u|)_+\). Therefore the box autocorrelation and signed Fubini give

\[
 Q(\mu):=E_{\rm res}/D_{\rm jump}
 =\frac1a\int_{\mathbb R}m(x)^2dx.
 \tag{10}
\]

Here \(|m|\leq\|\mu\|_{\rm TV}\) and its essential support is
\([0,T+a]\), so the energy is finite. Using closed intervals in both
places changes \(m\) only at a Lebesgue-null set of atom endpoints. The
half-open convention above makes the following partition literal.

Disintegrate the Lebesgue integral as

\[
 \int m(x)^2dx=\int_0^a\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}m(\theta+ja)^2d\theta.
 \tag{11}
\]

For each residue \(\theta\), the intervals
\((\theta+(j-1)a,\theta+ja]\) partition the line. Their signed masses
sum to one. Apart from the finitely many boundary residues that do not
affect (11), the only available slots are

\[
 \begin{cases}
 j=0,\ldots,n+1,&0<\theta<ra,\\
 j=0,\ldots,n,&ra<\theta<a.
 \end{cases}
\]

Some masses may be zero or negative; Cauchy still gives
\(\sum_{j=1}^N m_j^2\geq(\sum m_j)^2/N=1/N\). Integrating those two
families proves the all-measure lower

\[
 \boxed{Q(\mu)\geq q_*(L):=\frac{1-r}{n+1}+\frac r{n+2}>0.}
 \tag{12}
\]

There is no monotonicity premise in this argument. Finite total variation,
real scalar drive, compact time support and mass one are its control
hypotheses. At \(T=0\), the only measure is \(\delta_0\) and \(Q=1\).
At \(T=na\), the exceptional residue is zero and (12) is \(1/(n+1)\).
At \(r=1\), all nonboundary fibers have \(n+2\) slots and the answer is
\(1/(n+2)\), consistently.

The exact deficit form is also useful:

\[
 Q(\mu)-q_*(L)=\frac1a\int_0^a\sum_{j=1}^{N(\theta)}
       [m_j(\theta)-1/N(\theta)]^2d\theta.
 \tag{13}
\]

It locates any failure of equality without relying on a numerical optimizer.
